# Katana animation master

`KatanaCutVariants.blend` contains six authored clips on a 30 fps timeline. The two diagonals, right-to-left horizontal cut and overhead form the ordinary attack pool. Heavy Carry supplies the upper-body hold while the game drives locomotion. Charged Series supplies the charged action. Export samples the five attack clips at 240 fps and Carry at 30 fps, preserving their durations; the dense attack samples keep the two-hand grip through fast FK interpolation.

The blade is 186.56 cm long above its 11 cm heel. Its width increases above the collar while the handle, guard and both grip guides keep their dimensions. Carry and the shared guard raise the blade 18 degrees around the right grip, carrying both hand IK targets with it. Wide cuts move the rigid driver and both grips together: horizontal and diagonal tip bearings span -85 to +85 degrees; overhead cuts descend from 65 degrees elevation to a floor-limited endpoint. Inactive recovery routes the complete grip assembly in front of the belt. The source floor clearance is at least 4 cm through the six clips.

`Tools/Blender/widen_katana_swings.py` reproduces the wide pass from the retained pre-wide master or its prior source revision. One-based active frames are Right/Horizontal 7–14, Left 8–15, Overhead 9–17, and Charged 14–19 / 21–26 / 28–33. Geometry, Carry, guard endpoints, root translation, feet and facial curves are retained. Source IK contact error is below 0.0002 cm; simulated 240 fps FK interpolation stays below 0.71 cm and 1 degree. These authoring measurements do not establish runtime hit coverage.

Run `Tools/Blender/export_katana_gameplay.py` from Blender 5.2 with this master open and an explicit E: output directory. The bake keeps the gameplay gloves at their bind dimensions while placing the joints at the authored locations. Run `Tools/Unreal/import_katana_gameplay.py` in Unreal with `MC_KATANA_FBX_DIR` pointing to that directory. The import calibrates the sword mount curves against the Unreal hand transform. Run `Tools/Unreal/bind_katana_profile.py` to connect the clips to `DA_ToothAnimation` and `Tools/Unreal/validate_katana_assets.py` to check the imported poses.

In a development arena, open F3 and choose **Катана — себе**, then select slot 3. A primary press immediately requests an ordinary cut; up to two further presses buffer during recovery. The next cut is chosen uniformly from the other three variants. Continue holding primary for 0.38 s to automatically add one charged series; releasing does not request another attack. A held gesture produces at most one charged series. Replacing the tool or interrupting the action clears buffered input. The grant lasts until respawn. It occupies the prior chainsaw upgrade ID to preserve serialized references.

At the base attack rate, ordinary clips run for 0.367–0.417 s and their active cutting window begins at 0.0667 s; actual contact depends on target geometry. The private `MCKatanaPlayback.h` helpers keep animation, blade traces, facing and VFX on the same runtime clock. The charged action plays its complete 37/30-second authored clip at 1.4× speed: 0.881 s including preparation, all three active cuts and recovery. It begins at the guard, preserving the transition from the preceding ordinary cut; there is no separate preacceptance charge-pose preview. The first charged cutting window starts 0.3095 s after its acceptance, following the immediate ordinary hit. Public release-marker helpers remain for compatibility and source reference. A facing assist turns at most 20 degrees toward a target already within 40 degrees of forward, ends before the first cut, and yields to deliberate camera or movement input.

During each active cut, the server follows the blade's cutting edge. The edge must reach the enemy's gameplay collision body or the food mesh. The food cut plane passes through the contact point and follows the blade's orientation. A stroke cannot cut the pieces it just created. Each charged enemy stroke applies 1.15 times ordinary damage. A charged food contact opens hard shells through their existing fracture path; eligible cut portions become intake receipts when their complete geometry fits the front half-space, blade reach and vertical bounds, their cap touches the accepted contact, and static geometry does not obstruct them. Large or distant remainders keep ordinary cut behavior and mass accounting.
